# Handling Difficult Moments

### Nobody's perfect. Be ready.

Every shift has at least one curveball. The difference between a crisis and a story your guest tells later is **how you handle it in the first 90 seconds**.

### The Framework: Acknowledge → Act → Follow Up

| Step | What to Say | What NOT to Say |
|---|---|---|
| **Acknowledge** | "I hear you — that shouldn't have happened." | "That's not my fault." / "Are you sure?" |
| **Act** | "Let me fix this right now." | "I'll see what I can do." (vague = dismissive) |
| **Follow Up** | Come back within 5 minutes with a solution | Disappear and hope they forget |

### Common Scenarios

#### Wrong Order / Cold Food
- **Don't** explain why it happened (kitchen backed up, new cook, miscommunication)
- **Do** remove it immediately, apologize once, and get the replacement started before you say another word
- **Bonus**: Manager stops by the table within 3 minutes — a 20-second visit changes everything

#### Long Wait Time
- **Don't** hide from the table
- **Do** approach before they flag you down: "I know it's taking longer than it should. The kitchen is on it and it'll be out in [specific number] minutes."
- **Rule**: Under-promise, over-deliver. If they say 8 minutes, tell the guest 10.

#### Unhappy With Wine / Drink
- **Don't** argue about taste
- **Do** remove it, replace it, no questions asked
- **Cost to Richie's**: the pour cost of one glass. **Cost of an unhappy guest**: they never come back, and they tell 5 people.

#### Guest Had Too Much
- **Signal**: loud, unsteady, aggressive, or sleepy
- **Do**: Slow down service. Offer water, coffee, food. Alert the manager. Never serve another drink.
- **The law is clear**: you can be held personally liable. Protect the guest and protect yourself.

### The Principle

> **Fix first — debrief later.**

When something goes wrong, the guest doesn't care whose fault it is. They care whether it gets fixed. Fix it. Make them feel heard. Then — and only then — figure out what happened so it doesn't repeat.
